# Chapter 11: Main Discography and Research

## 11.1 Appreciation of Early LP and EP Records

Reilly's recording career dates back to the early 1950s: in 1951, he signed with the Parlophone label and produced a series of 78 rpm records with the young George Martin. Among them, *Deep Purple* (DeRose) is one of his earliest representative recordings (exact year to be confirmed, included in the 2019 Chandos centennial album *A Life in Music*), a choice that is symbolic: the work's popular status as a jazz standard creates a dialogue with Reilly's classical aspirations.

## 11.2 Studio Albums and EPs Era (1960—1985)

Before the CD era, Reilly recorded a significant number of studio albums and EPs for various major labels between 1960 and 1985, covering a wide range of styles including Irish folk, pop, Latin, classical, and film scores. The following information is based on the authoritative discography compiled by Sigmund Groven:

### EPs and Singles (1960—1963)

• 1960 Tivoli 43008 *The New Sound of Tommy Reilly* (College Girl, Blue Violets, Far, Fortune's Favourite) and 43009 *The Virtuosity of Tommy Reilly* (Never, Days of Yearning, Lotus, Someday Soon), with Bob Hueting conducting the band accompaniment;

• 1960 HOHNER T 4007: "The Navy Lark," "The Sailing Valse," "Before The Breeze," "Hoopla" (written by Reilly/Moody, James Moody on piano and rhythm section); T 7003: "Swiss Merry-go-round" (Reilly), "A la Francaise" (Martin), "No Limit" (Young), "Bulgarian Wedding Dance" (Moody);

• 1962 Philips BF 326 543: Blow, Man, Blow、No Dice (both composed by Reilly and Warren);

• 1963 Philips PB 1248: So Little Time, Moon Fire (theme from the film "55 Days at Peking", music by Tiomkin, with Ivor Raymonde Singers and Orchestra).

LP Albums (1965—1985)

• 1965 World Sound T 541 *The Life of Reilly* — 26 Irish folk songs, accompanied by James Moody and his band;

• 1967 Concorde ORL-ST 5002 *Chromonica Rallye mit Tommy Reilly* — collaboration with The Continentals (with *Flirt in Rio* credited to Reilly-Arnold, *No Time*, *Esmeralda*, *Tokyo* credited to Rundle-Morris — Reilly's pseudonym);

• 1968 Polydor 184 107 *Colours of My Life* — Original songs by David Reilly and Durham (Paper-hearted Friend, Colours of My Life, etc.), with classical arrangements including Bach's *Badinerie* and Stravinsky's *Chanson Russe*; produced by Groven in Norway in 1967;

• 1969 Polydor 222 002 *Melody Fair* — with Kai Warner singers and orchestra;

• 1970 Polydor 184 367 *Latin Harmonica* — with the Kai Warner Orchestra, 12 Latin classics;

• 1971 Polydor 2382 002 *The Harmonica of Tommy Reilly* - Classical album, with Kaare Ørnung on piano and string quartet (Scarlatti, Bach, Rachmaninoff's *Serenade*, Moszkowski, etc.); 2382 008 *The Music of Robert Farnon* - featuring the world premiere recording of *Prelude and Dance*; 2383 046 *Western Harmonica* - Western music album; Apollo Sound AS 1008 *Tommy Reilly Plays Fried Walter* - Album of works by Fried Walter (including the duet *Duettino* with Groven);

• 1972 Polydor 2382 016 *Harmonica Parisien* — French chanson album (later reissued by Philips as 6382 062);

• 1974 Polydor 2052 101: Joplin's *The Entertainer* and *Turkey Trot* (Kaare Ørnung piano);

• 1975 Philips 6382 081 *Warm Latin Sounds* — 12 Latin love songs; Argo ZDA 206 *The Silver Sound of the Harmonica* — Jacob's *Divertimento* and Moody's *Quintet*, with the Hindar Quartet;

• 1976 Polydor 2922 008 *Music for Two Harmonicas* - A duo harmonica album with Groven (Hindar Quartet, Skaila Kanga harp, David Reilly guitar), featuring Jacob's *Introduction and Galop*, Farnon's *Tête-à-Tête*, Moody's *Seventeen-Seventy-One*, Reilly's *Valsentino* and *The Rose of Telemark*; also recorded all harmonica parts for the Norwegian animated film *Flåklypa Grand Prix* (Bent Fabricius-Bjerre score, Polydor 2382 066);

• 1977 Argo ZRG 856—with the Academy of St Martin in the Fields, Marriner conducting, featuring Moody's *Little Suite*, Jacob's *Five Pieces*, Tausky's *Concertino*, Vaughan Williams' *Romance*;

• 1978 Argo ZK 55 *Harmonica Recital* — Moody piano, Kanga harp, 14 short pieces;

• 1979 Argo ZRG 905 *Tommy Reilly Plays Villa-Lobos Harmonica Concerto* — London Sinfonietta, Atherton conducting, featuring concertos by Villa-Lobos, Arnold, and Benjamin; also ZSW 613 *Tarka the Otter* film soundtrack (music by David Fanshawe);

• 1981 Philips 9500 997 *Romantic Melodies* — with Kanga harp duo (Bach-Gounod *Ave Maria*, Gounod *Faust* ballet music, Villa-Lobos *Bachianas Brasileiras* No. 5, etc.); Teldec 6.24965 AS *Winnetou Melodien* — with Martin Böttcher conducting the Munich Radio Orchestra, music from the German Western film series;

• 1985 Nova-Zembla NZR 85001 *Tommy Reilly and Pluche* — with the Pluche Salon Orchestra, arrangements by Moody (*Valencia*, *Falling in Love Again*, Shostakovich's *Romance* Op.97, etc.).

The above albums constitute Reilly's main recording legacy from the pre-CD era, with many of them being digitally remastered and reissued by labels such as Chandos.

### Entry Path: Three Albums

For first-time listeners of Reilly, it is recommended to listen in the following order:

1. **"Tommy Reilly Plays Harmonica Concertos and Virtuoso Works" (Chandos CHAN 9248, 1993)** - A work that simultaneously showcases the harmonica's "concerto capabilities" (complete concertos by Spivakovsky, Arnold, Villa-Lobos) and "virtuoso miniature tradition" (Toledo, Prelude and Dance). This is the best entry point to understand "how the harmonica converses with a symphony orchestra."

2. **"Serenade" (Chandos CHAN 8486, 1986)** - Collaborating with the Academy of St Martin in the Fields, showcasing the lyrical side of the harmonica: Fauré's Pavane, Grieg's Norwegian Dances, George Martin's Adagietto, Reilly's own "Serenade" and "My Lagan Love". Gramophone described it as "light, gentle, and unpretentious".

3. **"A Life in Music" (Chandos CHAN 20143, 2019)** - A centennial celebration compilation featuring 30 recordings spanning 1953-1988, from "Song of the Vagabond" to "Hora Staccato," from "Deep Purple" to "Bulgarian Wedding Dance." This album showcases his stylistic evolution over 35 years, with 15 tracks being released for the first time.

### Version Comparison: The Same Piece Across Different Eras

- **Valsentino**: 1976 "Music for Two Harmonicas" (collaborative version with Groven, guitarist positioned close to Reilly for rhythm synchronization) → 1992 "Serenade Vol.2" (with Kanga harp version). The latter's tempo and clarity set the benchmark for his later style.

- **Bulgarian Wedding Dance**: 1971 "The Harmonica of Tommy Reilly" → 1986 "Serenade". The evolution of tone from "sharper" to "warmer" can be heard over the 15 years.

- **Serenade (Rachmaninoff)**: 1971 on *The Harmonica of Tommy Reilly* (Ørnung piano) → 2019 compilation. Rachmaninoff's original was a vocal piece; Reilly's two recordings are a window into his concept of "modeling on the human voice."

### Listening Methodology: Listening Like Reilly

Uwe Warschkow notes that Reilly required his students to "listen critically to themselves," and the same approach applies to appreciating his recordings:

1. **Listen to the musical phrases first, then the technique**: Pay attention to how he handles the phrasing, breathing, and climax of the music, rather than focusing on speed first.

---

2. **Appreciate His Tone**: Tommy Reilly's tone is warm and expressive, which is a hallmark of his signature style.

2. **Distinguishing Blow and Draw**: He pursued throughout his life the goal that "listeners can never tell which note is blown and which is drawn" — when listening to his long phrases, try to find the breath points, and you will find that they are almost undetectable.

3. **Comparing the Same Piece Across Different Eras**: His vibrato evolved from the early "more intense, more violin-like" style to a later "more restrained, purer" approach (as observed by Zheng Zhangming).

4. **Listen with the sheet music**: The best way to learn his interpretation is to listen to the recordings while following the sheet music for Valsentino, Serenade, and Toledo (Franchbach engraved edition) included with this book.
