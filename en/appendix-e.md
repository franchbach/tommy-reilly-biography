# Appendix E Tommy Reilly on Performance: A Collection of Quotes

> Note: The following quotes are translated from the course transcript of Uwe Warschkow's "Harmonica Lessons with Tommy Reilly," BBC interviews, and Reilly's own accounts, all rendered into English. They are categorized into six themes, providing the most concise path to understanding Reilly's playing philosophy.

## E.1 The Nature of Music

1. "Music is not notes, music is emotion."

2. "No musician thinks note by note. Every musician thinks in phrases."

3. "A musical phrase is the smallest unit of music. Therefore, try to play an entire phrase in one fluid motion."

4. "They are the questions in the music, with the subsequent notes providing the answers."

5. "Etudes should also be played musically."

6. "If you practice the different parts of a piece with the utmost precision, you will gain a sense of the inherent tension within it, and then even if the score doesn't indicate it, you will naturally play it with dynamic variation."

## E.2 Harmonica Technical Philosophy

7. "The principle is: always take the shortest path, make the smallest possible movements."

8. "The head should always remain stable, only the harmonica needs to move."

9. "The tongue guides the instrument."

10. "When performing, you must never let the audience hear whether the note you are playing is blown or drawn."

11. "I think like a violinist, and I also phrase like a violinist."

12. "The slide should be pressed down fully and decisively."

13. "The dynamic range of the harmonica is not as wide as that of other wind instruments. Therefore, you must always pay special attention and consciously utilize the full dynamic range of the harmonica."

14. "Any tension will affect your playing."

## E.3 Legato and Phrasing

15. "When playing *Lullaby*, only when the performer can play legato, can they move the audience."

16. "Every note matters! You must sustain each note for its full duration."

17. "The ultimate effect should be: no one can tell when you are blowing or drawing. This is the most difficult challenge in harmonica playing. Once you master it, other musicians will accept you as an equal peer."

18. "Natural breathing points occur before you start a new musical phrase, giving shape to the phrase."

19. "If you take a breath in the middle of a musical phrase, it will most likely disrupt the arc of the music."

20. "Hum this phrase to find the best way to express its tension and how to play it on the harmonica."

## E.4 Methodology of Practice

21. "Never practice without concentration! It is not only a waste of time but also dangerous, as you may make such inaccuracies permanent."

22. "You should never practice like this: first practice bars 1 to 10, then bars 11 to 20, and so on. That is purely mechanical practice, devoid of any musical meaning."

23. "First, you need to identify the musical phrases: where it begins, where it climaxes, and where it ends."

24. "Listening to your own playing is the most important part of developing good playing technique."

25. "A good etude focuses on only one specific aspect of performance technique."

26. "Etudes are not an end in themselves; I suggest they should be matched with the pieces you are currently learning."

27. "When learning a new piece, don't just study the harmonica part. The entire work is important."

28. "A pianist would never make a big arc with their hand just to reach the next octave note."

## E.5 Teaching and Listening

29. "You're trying too hard, stay relaxed. Play it again."

30. "Correct movements are the foundation for developing good playing technique."

31. "We cannot overemphasize the importance of refining your movements."

32. "Never start without being mentally prepared."

33. "Always be mindful of maintaining the tempo. Amateur players often unconsciously play the louder passages faster and the softer passages slower."

34. "The contrast between strong and weak must be clearly audible."

35. "I don't want to be nitpicky, but I do want to refine your movements even more."

## E.6 Life and Attitude

36. "Those days in the prisoner-of-war camp are a part of my life, not five and a half years taken out of my life."

37. "Immersing myself in studying the harmonica also psychologically helped me to survive."

38. "I love this land. After returning from tours or escaping the hustle and bustle of the recording studios in London, I can find peace and relax here."

39. "What infuriates me is that Hohner changed the slide button without consulting me — such a decision could only come from someone who has never played the harmonica."

40. "For me, silver is the best material because it has proven its worth on the flute."

41. "The final push was when Robert Farnon composed *Prelude and Dance* for me. The piece was so technically and musically demanding that I concluded I couldn't meet these requirements with the standard Hohner 270."

42. "First and foremost, you are a musician, and only then a harmonica player."
